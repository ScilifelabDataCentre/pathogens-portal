_Pseudomonas aeruginosa_ is a gram-negative opportunistic pathogen that causes a wide range of infections, including urinary tract infections and chronic lung infections in cystic fibrosis patients. Beyond clinical settings, it is highly adaptable and persists in diverse environments such as rivers, drinking water systems, and swimming pools. One of its most widespread and successful lineages, clone C (sequence type ST17), is globally distributed across both environmental and clinical niches and is associated with acute and chronic infections.

To understand the ecological and pathogenic diversity of clone C, [Ham _et al._(2026)](https://doi.org/10.1128/mra.01240-25) generated complete genome sequences for three strains isolated from underrepresented environments. These included strain 8277 from a urinary tract infection in the United Kingdom, and strains PT31M (drinking water) and SG50M (swimming pool water) from anthropogenic aquatic environments in Germany. Prior to this work, complete genomes of clone C strains were largely limited to cystic fibrosis lung isolates, restricting comparative analyses across different ecological niches.

Genomic DNA was extracted from cultured cells and sequenced using a combination of PacBio SMRT long-read sequencing and Illumina NovaSeq short-read sequencing. Hybrid assembly approaches were applied to generate high-quality genomes, with long reads enabling de novo assembly and short reads used for error correction. The resulting assemblies were polished and annotated using established bioinformatics pipelines, ensuring accurate genome reconstruction and gene prediction.

All three strains were assembled into single circular chromosomes, with genome sizes ranging from 6.75 to 6.90 Mb and an average GC content of ~66.7%. The genomes contained between 6,156 and 6,318 predicted coding sequences, along with complete sets of rRNA and tRNA genes. Notably, no plasmids were detected; however, the commonly observed clone C mobile element pKLC102 was found integrated into the chromosome in all strains, indicating chromosomal incorporation of mobile genetic elements.

A key feature of all three genomes was the presence of the transmissible locus for stress tolerance (tLST), located within genomic islands. This locus encodes proteins such as the ClpG disaggregase and small heat shock proteins, which enhance bacterial survival under stress conditions. As illustrated in the genomic maps, the tLST region is somewhat highly conserved across the strains, while other genomic islands show variation, reflecting genomic plasticity and adaptation to different environments.

Comparative analysis revealed both shared and distinct genomic features among the strains. While all carried conserved stress tolerance mechanisms, differences in genomic islands beyond tLST suggest niche-specific adaptations between clinical and environmental isolates. For example, strain 8277 represents adaptation to a human urinary tract environment, whereas PT31M and SG50M reflect survival strategies in anthropogenic water systems.

By expanding the number of fully sequenced clone C genomes from diverse sources, [Ham _et al._(2026)](https://doi.org/10.1128/mra.01240-25) provide valuable reference data for studying the ecological versatility and global success of _P. aeruginosa_. Their findings highlight how genomic plasticity, combined with conserved stress tolerance mechanisms, enables clone C strains to thrive across both environmental reservoirs and human hosts. This work underscores the importance of including non-clinical isolates in genomic studies to better understand the transmission, persistence, and pathogenic potential of opportunistic bacteria.

- Whole-genome data have been deposited at GenBank under the accession numbers [CM129832](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/CM129832.1/) (PT31M), [CM129833](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/CM129833.1/) (SG50M), and [CP170200](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/CP170200) (8277).
- The associated BioProject and BioSample accession numbers are [PRJNA1321591](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/PRJNA1321591) and [SAMN51219634](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/biosample/SAMN51219634) (PT31M), [PRJNA1321598](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/PRJNA1321598) and [SAMN51219831](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/biosample/SAMN51219831) (SG50M), and [PRJNA1162667](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/bioproject/PRJNA1162667) and [SAMN43818467](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/biosample/SAMN43818467) (8277), respectively.
- The PacBio and Illumina sequencing raw data for the whole-genome data have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive database under the accession numbers [SRX30956583](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/?term=SRX30956583) and [SRX31989920](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/?term=SRR37043088) (PT31M), [SRX30956584](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRX30956584) and [SRX32015471](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRX32015471) (SG50M), and [SRX30950279](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRX30950279%5baccn%5d) and [SRX32015960](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/SRX32015960) (8277), respectively.
