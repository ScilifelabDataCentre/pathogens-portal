Carbapenem‑resistant Enterobacterales (CRE), especially Klebsiella pneumoniae and Escherichia coli, are major global health threats because they cause severe opportunistic infections and are often resistant to multiple antibiotic classes. To investigate a sudden appearance of CRE in outlet water from the Kristianstad wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) in 2024, researchers collected surface water samples from the treated effluent entering lake Hammarsjön and isolated carbapenem‑resistant colonies on selective agar. By using the over intensified sampling during September–October 2024, they obtained 55 CRE isolates, identified by MALDI‑ToF MS as 29 _E. coli_ and 26 _K. pneumoniae._

Real‑time PCR and melt‑curve analysis revealed that 94% of isolates carried bla<sub>NDM</sub> genes and 56% carried bla<sub>OXA‑48</sub> ‑ like genes, confirming widespread carbapenemase production in the effluent microbiota. Eighteen representative isolates (10 _E. coli_, 8 _K. pneumoniae_) were selected for whole‑genome sequencing to characterise sequence types, plasmids, virulence factors, and antibiotic resistance genes. Phylogenomic analysis showed that all sequenced isolates were multidrug‑resistant, each sequence type exhibiting conserved plasmid profiles and extensive resistance gene cargo.

The _E. coli_ isolates belonged to globally important high‑risk sequence types ST167, ST648, ST1284, and the emerging ST8346, whereas _K. pneumoniae_ isolates were ST437 and ST873. ResFinder analysis detected bla<sub>NDM‑5</sub> in all _E. coli_ and in _K. pneumoniae_ ST437, while co‑carriage of bla<sub>NDM‑5</sub> and bla<sub>OXA‑181</sub> together with extended-spectrum-β-lactamases (ESBL) gene bla<sub>CTX‑M‑15</sub> occurred in _K. pneumoniae_ ST437 and _E. coli_ ST8346, creating highly resistant clones. Although _K. pneumoniae_ ST437 carried virulence‑associated siderophore systems such as yersiniabactin, it lacked the full hypervirulence, indicating classic but still epidemiologically high‑risk strains.

Two _K. pneumoniae_ ST873 isolates from March 2024 were carbapenem‑intermediate or ‑resistant by disc diffusion but lacked known carbapenemase genes, harbouring only ESBL genes bla<sub>SHV‑110</sub> and bla<sub>CTX‑M‑15</sub>, suggesting alternative or yet‑unidentified resistance mechanisms. The _E. coli_ STs detected including ST167, ST648, ST1284, and ST8346 are all associated with urinary tract, bloodstream, and wound infections, highlighting the clinical relevance of the wastewater isolates. Notably, ST8346 has recently been reported as an emerging clone with plasmid‑borne co‑carriage of bla<sub>NDM‑5</sub> and bla<sub>OXA‑181</sub>, consistent with the genetic profiles observed here.

The Kristianstad wastewater treatment plant (WWTP) receives wastewater from approximately 210,000 people, the city hospital, and a large pig slaughterhouse, and had undergone more than a decade of surveillance (2014–2024) without detecting CRE before this event. The appearance of bla<sub>NDM‑5</sub>‑positive ST437 _K. pneumoniae_ and ST167 _E. coli_ in treated effluent therefore suggests a recent introduction and spread of high‑risk clones in the community that are not yet fully captured by clinical surveillance. Because WWTP inlets reflect carriage in the wider population and treated effluent can disseminate resistant bacteria into receiving waters, the findings imply an increased risk of environmental and potentially human transmission of carbapenem‑resistant pathogens.

By linking wastewater‑based surveillance with genomic characterisation, this study demonstrates how high‑risk CRE lineages and bla<sub>NDM‑5</sub> can silently establish in the environment even in countries with relatively low clinical incidence. It underscores the need for integrated One Health monitoring of resistance genes across clinical, community, and environmental compartments, and highlights WWTP effluents as critical control points for limiting the spread of multidrug‑resistant Enterobacterales.
