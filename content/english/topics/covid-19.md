## Background

COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus. COVID-19 was designated as a pandemic in early 2020, and went on to severely challenge global society. Most people suffer mild to moderate respiratory illness following infection, and recover without treatment. However, the disease can be severe or fatal. A severe outcome is more likely in the elderly, immunocompromised, and those with underlying medical conditions (e.g. cancer).

The global community collaborated and produced reearch at unprecedented levels during the COVID-19 pandemic. This enabled a greater level of understanding about the route of transmission, and action, of the virus more quickly, which was a crucial part of rapid vaccine development. The efforts of the research community reduced the impacts of the pandemic, and now we have entered a more endemic phase. There are many lessons learned from the pandemic that can help with future pandemic preparedness, and many research questions remain about COVID-19, too. For example, there is still an uncertainty regarding the duration of vaccine protection and effectiveness of booster doses. In addition, vaccine escape has been shown by several variants, which could make vaccines less effective.
