Ebola virus disease (EVD, formerly known as Ebola haemorrhagic fever), is a severe, often fatal, viral haemorrhagic disease caused by orthoebolaviruses (formerly ebolavirus). Humans contract EVD through contact with infected animals or bodily fluids from infected individuals, and can spread between humans through close physical contact. Symptoms typically include fever, fatigue, vomiting, diarrhea, and in severe cases, internal and external bleeding. More general information about EVD is available from the [World Health Organization (WHO)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/ebola-disease), [the European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC)](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/ebola-and-marburg-fevers), and [Folkhälsomyndigheten (FoHM).](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/vara-amnesomraden/smittsamma-sjukdomar/smittsam-sjukdom/sjukdomsinformation-om-ebola/)

In May 2026, an outbreak of EVD, caused by the rare Bundibugyo virus, was reported in the Ituri Province of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC), with additional imported cases detected in Uganda. The Bundibugyo virus is of particular concern because there are currently no approved vaccines or targeted treatments available. The outbreak has led to hundreds of suspected cases and a high rate of mortality, [prompting WHO to declare a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC)](https://www.who.int/news/item/17-05-2026-epidemic-of-ebola-disease-in-the-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-and-uganda-determined-a-public-health-emergency-of-international-concern).  
  
The area affected by the outbreak is also subject to conflict, population displacement, and limited healthcare infrastructure. This complicates surveillance and containment efforts. Several healthcare workers have also been infected, raising concerns about transmission within healthcare settings. Although the global risk is currently considered low, regional spread remains a concern due to cross-border travel and increasing case numbers in affected areas. More information about this particular outbreak is available from the [Africa Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (Africa CDC)](https://africacdc.org/news-item/africa-cdc-declares-the-ongoing-bundibugyo-ebola-outbreak-a-public-health-emergency-of-continental-security/), [ECDC](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/ebola-virus-disease-outbreak-democratic-republic-congo-19-may-2026), [Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (CDC)](https://www.cdc.gov/ebola/situation-summary/index.html), and [FoHM](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/vara-amnesomraden/sjukdomsutbrott/ebola-i-demokratiska-republiken-kongo-maj-2026/). 

The Swedish Pathogens Portal and [SciLifeLab’s Pandemic Laboratory Preparedness (PLP) program](https://www.pathogens.se/plp-program-background/) continues to monitor the outbreaks, in collaboration with the [BE READY Partnership](https://beready4pandemics.eu). Any outbreaks occurring in Sweden will be listed in [the portal’s dedicated section](https://swedish-pathogens-portal.scilifelab-2-dev.sys.kth.se/outbreaks/).
