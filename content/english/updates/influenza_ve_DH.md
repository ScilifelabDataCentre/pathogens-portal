Our latest [data highlight](/highlights/) titled ‘[exploring the influenza vaccine effectiveness in European children](/highlights/influenza_vaccine_effectiveness/)’, presents the work of [Lucaccioni _et al._ (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2025-072907). The study evaluates how well influenza vaccines protected children attending primary care across Europe during the 2022-2023 and 2023-2024 seasons.

[Lucaccioni _et al._ (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2025-072907) analysed data from over 19,000 pediatric patients across multiple European countries using a test-negative case-control design. The study found that influenza vaccination provided substantial protection, with more than two-thirds of vaccinated children protected against medically attended influenza in both seasons. Vaccine effectiveness (VE) was estimated at 68% in 2022-2023 and 71% in 2023-2024 against any influenza infection .

The study also revealed important differences by virus subtype. Protection was highest against influenza B (up to 92%) and strong against A(H1N1)pdm09, while lower effectiveness was observed for A(H3N2), reflecting known challenges with this subtype. VE varied across age groups, with generally higher protection observed in younger children.

These findings underscore the importance of continuous monitoring of influenza vaccine effectiveness in pediatric populations. They also highlight the potential to further reduce disease burden by increasing vaccination coverage, particularly among high-risk groups.

This work provides valuable insights for public health decision-making, vaccine strategy optimization, and infectious disease surveillance. Researchers and policymakers working on vaccination programs, pediatric infectious diseases, and epidemiology will find this study particularly relevant.
