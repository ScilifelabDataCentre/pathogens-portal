The latest editorial "[From Fragmented Data to Preparedness: Why Sweden Needs a Stronger Research Infrastructure for Infection Data](editorials/critical_microbes)" explores an important challenge for infectious disease research in Sweden; how to connect microbiology data generated across the country to better understand infection burden, patient outcomes and public-health impact.

Sweden generates extensive clinical microbiology and health data, but this information is distributed across 21 healthcare regions and multiple registries. Differences in data structures, terminology, diagnostic methods and reporting systems make it difficult to build a harmonised national picture. The editorial discusses how these gaps can affect research on disease severity, antimicrobial resistance and infection burden, and highlights the importance of sustainable data infrastructure, common standards, and better interoperability.

A central focus of the editorial is [CRITICAL MICROBES (Comprehensive Research on InfecTIons Complications Across the Lifespan – MICROBiology Epidemiology in Sweden)](/resources/critical_microbes/). The initiative aims to develop a research infrastructure that brings together clinical microbiology results and links them with patient characteristics and health outcomes. It also explores approaches for harmonising heterogeneous historical data, building on national standardisation efforts, and experience from Danish laboratory-data infrastructures such as [Danish Microbiology Database (MiBa)](https://mibaen.ssi.dk/miba) and LABKA research database ([Grann _et al.,_ 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3072155/)).

The editorial highlights that better connected microbiology data can strengthen both infectious disease research and preparedness. By bringing together data, clinical expertise, harmonising methods, and collaboration across institutions, CRITICAL MICROBES aims to help transform routinely generated microbiology information into research-ready evidence and improve our understanding of infections across Sweden.
