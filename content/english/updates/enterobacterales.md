Our latest data highlight ‘[Multidrug‑resistant Enterobacterales in Swedish Wastewater Effluent](/highlights/enterobacterales/)’ is focused on the work of [Axelsson _et al._ (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0950268826101071), which explores the investigation of Carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE). Axelsson _et al._ (2026) documented the first detection of bla<sub>NDM-5</sub> carbapenemase-producing high-risk clones in treated wastewater effluent from Kristianstad wastewater treatment plant (WWTP), signalling silent community spread despite prior surveillance showing no CRE. Sequencing data from eighteen representative isolates has been deposited in public repositories for broader One Health analysis.

The research integrates culture-based screening, real-time PCR, MALDI-TOF identification, and whole-genome sequencing of 55 CRE isolates (29 _E. coli_, 26 _K. pneumoniae_) collected September–October 2024. Key findings identify globally threatening sequence types: _E. coli_ ST167/ST648/ST1284/ST8346 and _K. pneumoniae_ ST437/ST873 carrying bla<sub>NDM-5</sub> (often co-located with bla<sub>OXA-181</sub> or bla<sub>CTX-M-15</sub>) exhibit multidrug resistance and possess virulence factors such as yersiniabactin. After a decade without CRE detections, this sudden emergence in hospital-influenced effluent entering lake Hammarsjön underscores wastewater as a sentinel for tracking resistant pathogens escaping clinical surveillance.

This work offers critical insights for antimicrobial resistance monitoring and environmental microbiology, revealing how high-risk CRE silently infiltrate aquatic systems in low-prevalence settings. Researchers investigating One Health surveillance, plasmid-mediated resistance spread, wastewater epidemiology, or high-risk clonal dynamics will find this resource particularly valuable.
