Since mid-August 2026, multiple cases of measles have been reported in Sweden, with the majority linked to the Urkult festival held in Västernorrland between 30 July and 2 August 2026, according to the [Public Health Agency of Sweden (Folkhälsomyndigheten, FoHM)(available only in Swedish)](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/vara-amnesomraden/sjukdomsutbrott/massling-i-sverige-augusti-2026/).

The first update, published on 15 August, described five confirmed cases, three of which had a likely epidemiological link to the festival. As case finding and contact tracing progressed, the number of confirmed cases increased to 10 by 17 August, and to 31 by 25 August. Cases have been reported from nine Swedish regions, indicating secondary spread following exposure at the festival.

Most cases have occurred among school-aged children and young adults, and the majority were unvaccinated against measles. Regional communicable disease control units are conducting extensive contact tracing and advising festival attendees to seek medical advice if symptoms develop.

[Measles](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/vara-amnesomraden/sjukdomsutbrott/massling-i-sverige-augusti-2026/om-massling-i-korthet/) is one of the world's most contagious viral diseases and spreads through airborne respiratory droplets. An infected individual can transmit the virus from four days before until four days after the appearance of the characteristic rash, and susceptible individuals may become infected even after entering a room up to two hours after an infectious person has left. Despite the current outbreak, Sweden maintains a high vaccination coverage of approximately 95%, and outbreaks remain uncommon due to the national childhood immunisation programme. 

The latest information about this outbreak is available from [Folkhälsomyndigheten (available only in Swedish)](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/vara-amnesomraden/sjukdomsutbrott/massling-i-sverige-augusti-2026/) and on our [_Measles_ page within the outbreaks section](/pathogens/measles_26).

The Swedish Pathogens Portal continues to monitor this outbreak and provide updates as new information becomes available.
