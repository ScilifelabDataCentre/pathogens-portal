An outbreak of [hepatitis A](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/smittsamma-sjukdomar/sjukdomsinformation-om-hepatit-a/) (genotype IA) is currently under investigation in Sweden. Since early September, five confirmed cases (three men and two women) aged 17 to 64 have been reported across three regions. All affected individuals consumed frozen imported strawberries and raspberries that had not been heated before eating.

Hepatitis A is a viral infection that causes inflammation of the liver, with symptoms ranging from fever and nausea to jaundice and fatigue. While the illness typically resolves without long-term damage and does not become chronic, individuals with underlying liver conditions may be at increased risk of complications. Recovery generally results in lifelong immunity.

Although the suspected source is the berries, laboratory tests have not confirmed the presence of the virus in the sampled products. The latest reported illness began on October 23. The investigation is being conducted collaboratively by regional infectious disease control units, municipalities, the Swedish National Food Agency, and the Public Health Agency of Sweden.

Travelers to areas where hepatitis A is common are advised to get vaccinated. Two doses provide protection lasting at least 25 years.

The Swedish Pathogens Portal continues to monitor and investigate research resources linked to outbreaks and emergent pathogens. Information on them is added to our [emerging pathogens section](https://www.pathogens.se/pathogens/) as soon as possible. Where appropriate, the portal will also create other resources in order to accelerate research in the area.
