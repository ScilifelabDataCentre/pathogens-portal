We have published a new editorial in our [Editorials section](https://www.pathogens.se/editorials/). Our editorials are short, opinion-style pieces reflecting the ‘current thinking’ on a topic related to infectious disease and pandemic preparedness.

This latest editorial, entitled “[_Influenza A(H3N2) subclade K: when “seasonal” stops being predictable_](editorials/influenza_subclade_k)” discusses how the rapid rise of influenza A(H3N2) **_subclade K_** (also called **_J.2.4.1_**) is a reminder that “seasonal flu” is not predictable or stable. While the emergence of new H3N2 subclades is normal, the problem is that influenza evolves faster than our vaccine system can adapt, because strain selection happens about six months before the season.

The focus of this Editorial is a discussion on how we can strategically approach the influenza drift. The author covers key areas of focus as increased vaccine uptake in high-risk groups, strengthening rapid testing along with early antiviral treatments and modernizing the influenza vaccine system.

The editorial was written by Davide Angeletti, Professor at the [Department of Microbiology and Immunology](https://www.gu.se/en/about/find-organisation/department-of-microbiology-and-immunology) in University of Gothenburg.
