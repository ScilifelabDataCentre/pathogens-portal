## Background 

- **Pathogen** : [Hepatitis A](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/smittsamma-sjukdomar/sjukdomsinformation-om-hepatit-a/) is a form of viral hepatitis caused by an RNA virus of the _picornavirus_ family. It is an infectious liver disease that leads to liver inflammation ranging from mild to severe symptoms. 
Hepatitis A causes symptoms such as fever, nausea, and sometimes vomiting. As the illness progresses, some individuals may develop jaundice, where the skin and the whites of the eyes turn yellow. Other signs can include dark, reddish-brown urine and pale, clay-colored stools. While not all patients develop jaundice, fatigue and loss of appetite are common and may persist for several weeks or even months.
The infection generally resolves on its own without causing lasting liver damage and does not become chronic. The mortality rate is low, remaining under one percent. Importantly, recovery from the disease confers lifelong immunity. For travelers heading to regions where hepatitis A is common, vaccination is recommended. Two doses provide protection that lasts for at least 25 years.

- **Transmission**: The virus is commonly transmitted from person to person but can also spread through contaminated food and water.

- **Sources and risk factors**:  The source in this particular outbreak is contaminated food (contaminated imported berries). Individuals suffering from chronic liver disease or have underlying liver conditions are at a greater risk of developing complications. 


- **Seasonality**: In Sweden, there is a [persistent seasonal peak season in autumn from September to November that is observed historically](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/HEPA_AER_2022_Report.pdf#:~:text=Hepatitis%20A%20typically%20has%20a%20seasonal%20pattern,in%20case%20numbers%20was%20evident%20in%20September.) likely linked to travel abroad to endemic areas.


## Brief Summary of the Outbreak

As of November 20, 2025, [an outbreak of hepatitis A (genotype IA) is under investigation in Sweden](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/sjukdomsutbrott/hepatit-a-sverige-september-2025/), with five confirmed cases reported since early September across three regions. The affected individuals (three men and two women) aged 17 to 64 all consumed frozen imported berries, strawberries and raspberries. The berries were consumed raw (uncooked). Although the berries are suspected to be the source, lab tests have not detected hepatitis A virus in the sampled berries. The most recent illness onset was on October 23.

The investigation is a joint effort involving regional infectious disease control departments, municipalities, the [Swedish National Food Agency](https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/en/), and the [Public Health Agency of Sweden](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/).
