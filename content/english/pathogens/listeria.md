## Background 

- **Pathogen** : The causative agent is the bacterium [*Listeria monocytogenes*](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/smittsamma-sjukdomar/listeriainfektion/). It is widely present naturally in the environment,and in the intestines of many mammals, including humans.

- **Transmission**: Infection is almost always [foodborne](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/smittsamma-sjukdomar/listeriainfektion/): ingestion of contaminated food in which the bacterium has grown under favourable conditions (for example cold storage, long shelf‑life). [](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/smittsamma-sjukdomar/listeriainfektion/)Human‑to‑human transmission is very rare, except from mother to fetus/infant during pregnancy or childbirth.

- **Sources and risk factors**: Typical sources include ready‑to‑eat foods, refrigerated foods with long shelf‑life, smoked fish, soft cheeses, vacuum‑packed cold‑cuts, and other chilled foods where *Listeria monocytogenes* can multiply slowly. High risk group for serious illness typically include older age, immunocompromised status, and pregnancy (with both mother and fetuses at risk).

- **Seasonality**: No seasonality to infections. *Listeria monocytogenes* can grow at refrigeration temperatures. Food storage conditions (especially in chilled foods) matter more than classical seasonal weather patterns.

## Brief History of the Outbreak

Since the end of September 2025, a number of people have fallen ill with confirmed *Listeria monocytogenes* infections after eating at the same restaurant in Stockholm. The regional public health authority (Smittskydd Stockholm) is investigating the outbreak, and Folkhälsomyndigheten is coordinating national‑level work, including genetic analyses of isolates. [](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/utbrott/aktuella-utbrott/listeria-stockholm-september-2025/)

Visits to the restaurant resulting in infection occurred over a short period (23–27 September). A total of 15 confirmed cases (individuals with *Listeria monocytogenes* in their blood) have been identified as of (DATE); two of those isolates that have been sequenced are genetically identical (same sequence type) and not previously seen in the national database. Environmental/food source investigations are ongoing. [](https://www.folkhalsomyndigheten.se/smittskydd-beredskap/utbrott/aktuella-utbrott/listeria-stockholm-september-2025/)

Whilst *Listeria monocytogenes* typically causes serious illness in high risk group. In this case, on the contrary healthy people have become ill, with some having developed sepsis (blood‑infection) soon after exposure. This means that the outbreak is considered unusual for *Listeria monocytogenes* in Sweden.
